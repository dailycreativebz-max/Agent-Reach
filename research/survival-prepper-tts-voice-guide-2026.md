# TTS Voice Guide — Survival Prepper Niche 2026

**Version:** 1.0
**Companion to:** *Survival Prepper Niche Report 2026 (v1.1)* — this guide operationalizes the channel's voice for text-to-speech narration across all five ranked video packages, Shorts companions, and any breaking-news updates.
**How to use:** Sections 2–4 define the voice. Sections 5–8 are paste-ready prompts for your TTS tool. Sections 9–12 cover per-video application, auditioning, and rejection criteria.

---

## 1. Why This Voice Wins in This Niche

The niche's biggest content failure mode is **doomer hysteria** — breathless "GRID DOWN TOMORROW" delivery that triggers YouTube's misinformation dampening and burns viewer trust. The report's corrected positioning (measured claims: "NERC's 3rd-Ever Level 3 Alert," not "Highest Alert Ever") demands a voice that matches. The winning position is:

> **Calm authority under real stakes.** A voice that sounds like it has handled emergencies before and isn't impressed by them.

Four audience facts drive the spec:

1. **The audience skews 40–65+, male, rural/suburban.** Older ears prefer slower, lower-pitched delivery (age-related high-frequency hearing loss starts at 2 kHz+). Fast talkers and sibilant voices cause churn and "can't understand him" comments.
2. **Prime viewing is evening, often on TV/living-room speakers** (the report's 2 PM ET posting slot = 8–9 PM SAST for you, prime-time US evening consumption later). The voice must stay intelligible at low volume on mediocre speakers — mid-frequency energy, not crisp studio highs.
3. **~24.5M American preppers already trust plain-spoken sources** — the most-watched channels in this space win on credibility, not production polish. A TTS channel starts with a trust deficit; the voice is the #1 lever to close it.
4. **Differentiation:** most AI-narrated prepper channels use generic "documentary narrator" or hyped "military trailer voice." Sounding like a *steady, specific person* is the moat.

## 2. Core Voice Profile — The One-Paragraph Spec

> **Masculine, perceived age 45–55, General American accent with a touch of rural warmth.** Deep, warm baritone with chest resonance — weathered but healthy, like a retired utility engineer who's given a thousand briefings. **Unhurried: 140–150 words per minute.** Concerned where concern is earned, never alarmist. States hard facts flat and lets them land. Authority comes from falling intonation and patient pauses, never volume. The overall effect: *the steady neighbor who already did the thing he's describing.*

**Archetype:** Retired grid engineer × scoutmaster. Not a drill sergeant, not a news anchor, not a guru.

## 3. Detailed Characteristics

| Attribute | Specification | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| **Gender presentation** | Masculine | Matches audience majority and niche convention; deviation would need deliberate strategy (see §10) |
| **Perceived age** | 45–55 | Old enough for lived authority, young enough for 10+ years of channel runway |
| **Pitch** | Low-normal to baritone, ~95–115 Hz fundamental | Lower pitch reads as credible and calm; carries on TV speakers |
| **Timbre** | Warm, dry, lightly weathered. "Broken-in leather, not gravel" | Weathering = experience; gravel = alarm/age parody |
| **Resonance** | Chest, forward placement | Intelligible at low volume without brightness fatigue |
| **Sibilance** | Moderate and soft — crisp S, never hissy | Aging ears + cheap speakers punish sibilance |
| **Accent** | General American, non-regional; faint rural warmth allowed | Broad US reach; no strong drawl (narrows and stereotypes the channel) |
| **Energy baseline** | 6/10 | Room to rise for hooks and steps without peaking into hype |
| **Pace** | 140–150 wpm long-form (see §5 matrix) | ~10% slower than typical YouTube narration; matches 40+ audience |
| **Dynamic range** | Narrow–moderate | Big swings sound performative; consistency sounds honest |
| **Breath** | Audible occasionally, controlled | Occasional breath = human; constant breathlessness = panic |
| **Emotional read** | Protective, plainspoken, dry humor allowed (rare, 1× per video max) | Community warmth drives the "steady neighbor" parasocial bond |

## 4. Tone Map — By Video Section

The voice shifts **within** a video. Script these shifts explicitly (most TTS tools honor them via sentence structure and punctuation more than markup).

| Section | Tone | Delivery note |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook (0–15 s)** | Controlled concern | Slightly quicker, slight energy lift, but NO urgency spike. Land the stake in the first sentence, flat |
| **Evidence / data** | Neutral, precise | Slow 5–10%. Falling intonation on every stat. 300–400 ms pause after each number |
| **"Here's what it means"** | Thoughtful | Mid pace, warmer. This is where trust is built |
| **Action steps / list** | Encouraging, competent | Quicken 5–10%. Crisp itemization; slight lift per item |
| **Objection handling** | Patient, respectful | Never defensive. Slower, level |
| **CTA / close** | Warm, level, community-toned | "Take care of your people." Zero salesmanship |

**The pivot rule:** the single most important tonal moment is the pivot from problem to plan ("That's the bad news. Here's the good news —"). Insert a ~500 ms pause before it, then brighten slightly. This one beat is what separates "preparedness education" from "doom content."

## 6. Micro-Delivery Rules

**Numbers (the niche is full of them — $490, 265,000 cal, $1.36/meal, 230 kV, 1,500 MW):**
- Slow down ~15% on any figure; brief pause before and after.
- In scripts, write prices as "490 dollars" on first mention (guarantees correct TTS read); "$490" is fine afterward.
- Calorie counts: "265,000 calories" — write with the comma; most TTS engines read it correctly. Verify in render.
- Ranges: write "seven to eleven percent," never "7–11%."

**Acronyms & technical terms** (script them phonetically on first use):
- NERC → "Nerk" (spell it *NERC — pronounced Nerk* in your notes; most engines say "nurk," close enough — verify)
- kV → "kilovolt"; MW → "megawatts"; GW → "gigawatts"
- PJM → "P-J-M"; NoVA → "Northern Virginia"
- "load shed," "blackstart," "base load" — natural familiarity, no vocal quotes around jargon

**Emphasis:**
- Emphasize with a *slowdown + slight volume bump*, never a pitch spike. Pitch spikes sound like selling.
- One emphasized phrase per paragraph, maximum.

**Questions to viewer:**
- Rare (1–2 per video), genuine, slightly warmer pitch. "So what does that mean for your kitchen table?" — then pause 400 ms.

**Breathing & pauses:**
- 250–400 ms after data points; ~500 ms before problem→plan pivots; beat between list items.
- Prefer TTS pause controls (break tags / punctuation) over hoping the engine infers them.

## 7. Writing for TTS — Script Formatting Rules

These make ANY engine sound better and follow directly from the report's scripts:

1. **Sentences ≤ 18 words.** One idea per sentence. TTS handles short declaratives best.
2. **Comma = breath, period = beat.** Use them deliberately for pacing.
3. **No semicolons** — most engines misread them. Split into two sentences.
4. **No ALL CAPS, no stacked exclamation marks** — engines interpret them as intensity and will shout. The one exception: a single "!" per script maximum, if ever.
5. **Em-dashes → periods.** Engines render em-dashes inconsistently (some as nothing, some as a drag).
6. **No parentheticals** — rewrite as their own sentence.
7. **Ellipses: max one per 300 words** — engines render them as a long, spooky drag. Overuse is the #1 cause of accidental-doomer TTS.
8. **Homographs to watch in this niche:** *live* (wire) vs *live* (living) → write "a LIVE wire — that's L-I-V-E" or restructure; *lead* the metal (prepper contexts) → "lead, the metal"; *read* past tense → restructure.
9. **Spell out dates:** "August third, twenty twenty-six," not "Aug 3, 2026."
10. **Acronym expansion on first use** is optional on-screen (title card), but the VO should say the acronym naturally per §6.

## 8. Paste-Ready Prompts

### 8a. Short — single-line voice-description field (~30 words)

> Mid-50s American man, deep warm baritone, calm and unhurried, plainspoken rural steadiness, trusted-neighbor authority, measured pacing, clear diction, understated urgency.

### 8b. Medium — style/direction prompt (~110 words)

> Male, late 40s to mid 50s, General American accent, deep warm baritone with chest resonance. Calm, measured, unhurried — about 145 words per minute. You are a steady neighbor who has handled emergencies before: a retired utility engineer crossed with a scoutmaster. You state hard facts flat and let them land, with a short pause after every number. Concerned where concern is earned, never alarmist. Authority comes from falling intonation and patient pauses, never from volume. Action steps brighten slightly — encouraging, competent. You close warm and level. Never shout, never whisper, never upsell.

### 8c. Full — system-prompt-grade voice bible (~240 words)

> You are the voice of a preparedness channel for everyday Americans. Male, mid-40s to mid-50s, General American accent with a hint of rural warmth. Deep, warm baritone — chest resonance, not throat-growl. Weathered but healthy: a voice that has given a thousand briefings, not one that has screamed itself hoarse.
>
> Delivery is calm, measured, unhurried: 140–150 words per minute. You sound like the steady neighbor who already did the thing he is describing. Concerned where concern is earned, but never alarmist. You state hard facts flat and let them land; you do not dramatize them. Authority comes from falling intonation and unhurried pauses, never from volume.
>
> Numbers are spoken slowly and cleanly, with a short beat after each one. Technical terms are familiar, not foreign — you have said "NERC," "kilovolt," and "load shed" a thousand times, and none of them get special announcement treatment. Questions to the viewer are rare, genuine, and slightly warmer in pitch. Transitions to action steps brighten slightly: encouraging, competent, in control.
>
> You never: shout, whisper, upsell, rush, sigh dramatically, or use influencer cadence. No "hey guys," no vocal fry for emphasis, no sing-song sales lilt, no dramatic movie-trailer gravitas.
>
> You close warm and level: "Take care of your people. I'll see you in the next one."

### 8d. Negative prompt (for tools that support "avoid" lists)

> Avoid: shouting, hype, doomer panic, breathy ASMR whisper, young influencer cadence, infomercial upsell tone, robotic monotone, heavy Southern drawl, gravelly tactical tough-guy growl, movie-trailer gravitas, sing-song narration.

### 8e. Per-format style add-ons (append to 8b/8c)

- **News-explainer:** "Today you are delivering a sober briefing. Slowest setting, minimal pitch variation, a full beat of silence after every statistic."
- **Budget build:** "Today you are walking me through a hands-on shopping and packing session. Quietly enthusiastic, practical, warm."
- **Shorts:** "Urgent but composed. Hit the first clause hard, no pauses until the final call to action. Ten percent faster than your usual."
- **Breaking update:** "Slowest and gravest. Something real happened. No music-video energy — the calmest person in the room."

## 9. Applying It to the Report's Video Packages

| Package | Voice mode | Key moments |
|---|---|---|
| **V1 — NERC Level 3 / grid strain** (RANK #1) | Sober briefing | Title stat ("third Level 3 alert in 58 years") delivered FLAT — the fact is dramatic enough. The 7 actions get the brightened, competent read |
| **V2 — $490 / 30-day stockpile** (RANK #2) | Hands-on warmth | Every price gets its micro-pause; "exactly 490 dollars" lands as quiet satisfaction, not triumph |
| **V3–V5** | Per §5 matrix | Match the format (listicle vs build vs explain) |
| **Shorts companions** | Compressed urgency | First clause = hook, spoken at 170 wpm; still zero shouting |
| **Breaking updates** (grid events in the news cycle) | Gravest, slowest | This is the payoff of never crying wolf: when something real happens, the channel that was calm all along gets believed |

**Consistency rule:** lock ONE voice and ONE settings preset for all long-form. Channel identity = voice identity for a faceless channel. Never mix two primary voices in one video.

## 10. Optional Secondary Voice (Series B)

For pantry-cooking, taste-test, and food-preservation content (where the report notes women are a thin-but-present audience segment), a secondary voice lets you launch a distinct series without diluting the main channel:

> **Feminine, 38–50, mezzo voice, "master gardener / homesteader" energy.** Warm, organized, slightly quicker (148–155 wpm). Same absolute rule: no panic, no upsell. Reads as the capable neighbor who cans 200 jars a year and thinks that's normal.

Use it only for a clearly-branded sub-series (e.g., "The Pantry Lab"), never interleaved with the main voice in the same video.

## 11. Audition Protocol

Test every candidate voice with the **same 45-word script** (drawn from the niche, exercising numbers, a proper noun, a reassurance pivot, and a question):

> "NERC just issued its third Level 3 alert in fifty-eight years. And for the first time, it points straight at data centers. Should you panic? No. Should you prepare? Absolutely. Thirty days of food costs about 490 dollars. Let me show you the list."

**Score each candidate 1–5 on:**
1. Numbers clean and unhurried (58, 490)
2. "NERC" pronounced naturally, not spelled out
3. Calm-under-urgency in the panic/prepare exchange
4. Warmth (would you take advice from this voice?)
5. Perceived age 45–55
6. Pace stability — doesn't sprint at the end
7. Pause discipline around statistics
8. Zero sales-y inflection anywhere
9. Sibilance tolerable on a phone speaker at 50% volume
10. Gut check: "Would I trust this person with my family's emergency plan?"

Accept only candidates scoring 40+. Reject on any single catastrophic failure (shouting, whisper, upsell).

## 12. Anti-Patterns — The Reject List

| Voice type | Why it fails here |
|---|---|
| **Doomer hype** ("GRID DOWN TOMORROW!!") | Triggers misinfo dampening; burns the calm-authority moat; alienates the 40+ audience |
| **Movie-trailer gravitas** | Reads as entertainment, not information; undermines credibility of corrected, precise claims |
| **Young influencer cadence** ("Hey guys, welcome back!") | Age and trust mismatch with the audience |
| **ASMR whisper** | Wrong emotional register; reads as creepy, not protective |
| **Tactical tough-guy growl** | Stereotypes the niche; narrows to a subset and invites platform moderation |
| **Neutral corporate narrator** | Bland; no parasocial bond; indistinguishable from every other AI channel |
| **News anchor polish** | Feels institutional/broadcast, not neighborly — the report's positioning is "one of us who did the homework" |

## 13. Practical Notes for a TTS-Narrated Channel

- **Disclosure:** check YouTube's current synthetic-media disclosure requirements and tick "altered/synthetic content" where realistic TTS narration applies. It costs nothing in this niche and protects the channel.
- **Over-index on visible sourcing.** A voice channel closes its trust deficit with on-screen citations (the report's Section H checklist already requires this — date-stamped screenshots of NERC/FEMA/BLS sources in every video).
- **Keep the render preset saved** (voice + stability/style settings). Re-rendering an old video later with a different voice quietly destroys continuity for returning viewers.
- **Voice-first thumbnail/branding decisions.** Once the voice is locked, let the verbal close ("Take care of your people") become the channel's verbal signature — a consistency asset no doomer channel can copy without sounding like a parody.
